About

Welcome. And thanks for your interest in my work.

As a tenured professor and poet, my passion for literature was sparked by the irreverence of the trickster, specifically the evening tales of my Lagos childhood, where clever turtles and characters like Anansi constantly outwitted more powerful adversaries. Those stories followed me to Ibadan, Nigeria’s literary capital, where I trained in English and, later, performance studies. And then, to the Canadian prairies, where I received a PhD in English at the University of Saskatchewan. Today, with screens, algorithms, social media, and AI systems in the mix, I study how contemporary African narratives and cultural productions circulate in the wired and unruly ecologies of digital media.

My research focuses on the role of the digital in African literary and cultural studies, as well as the creation of digital cultural records and the use of computational methods to analyze African popular texts, Nollywood posters, and internet memes.

Projects

I have also edited or co-edited special journal issues, including a commemorative edition on the EndSARS Movement in Nigeria, in which I explore the afterlives of hashtags and digital activism, and an African Digital Humanities issue of Reviews in Digital Humanities (2024), as well as a 2020 issue of the Journal of African Cultural Studies on Túndé Kèlání and global cinema. I have published my other work in Digital Studies / Le champ numérique, African Studies Review, Digital Scholarship in the Humanities, and the Journal of the African Literature Association (JALA), among others. Forthcoming essays will appear in the Journal of West African History and Public Humanities (Cambridge University Press).

I also experiment with a couple of digital projects, including an Omeka-based project Digital Nollywood, a web-based archive of Nollywood film posters, and a digital scholarly edition of Onitsha Market literary pamphlets, using minimal computing platforms. With Jumoke Verissimo, I also co-created Soro Soke: When Poetry Speaks Up, a collaborative digital project on #EndSARS (Brittle Paper, 2020). You may also find me on Substack where I write mostly literary and cultural essays on football. Consider this part side hustle, part public commentary on the Beautiful Game, and mostly an excuse to watch matches and call it research.

Teaching, editing and service

I lead African digital humanities initiatives at the University of Kansas (africandh.ku.edu) and co-organize the annual African Digital Humanities Symposium, held with the University of Ghana, and the African Digital Indaba series.

I teach courses in African literature, postcolonial digital humanities, Black Masculinities, social media and African popular culture, and critical AI and cultural studies. I was managing editor of Africana Annual from 2022 to 2026. I am reviews editor of African Studies Review, and I become an associate editor of the journal in May 2027. I also serve on the editorial boards of the Nordic Journal of African Studies, Journal of African Cultural Studies, and Hyperrhiz: New Media Cultures, and on the Board of Directors of the African Studies Association (2024–2027).
